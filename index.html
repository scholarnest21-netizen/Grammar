Overview: Transformation of Sentences
17.1 — Core Concept
When a sentence is changed from one grammatical form to another without changing its meaning, it is called Transformation of Sentence. This is the natural habit of human communication — an attempt to know the procedures by which a sentence is re-expressed while keeping its meaning intact.

Direct & Indirect Speech
Narration — the most tested transformation
💬 Direct Speech
Reproduces the actual words of the speaker in quotation marks.

Reporting Verb + "Reported Speech"
Example
He said, "I want to go to a picnic."
📝 Indirect Speech
Gives the substance of the speaker's words — no quotation marks, joined by "that".

Reporting Verb that Reported Speech
Example
He said that he wanted to go to a picnic.
Narration transformation flow: Direct → Indirect Speech
📋 Rules for Transformation (Direct → Indirect)
  • iThe reporting verb and reported speech are joined by "that".
  • iiThe tense of the reporting verb remains the same.
  • iiiIf the reporting verb is in present or future tense, the tense of reported speech does not change.
  • ivIf the reporting verb is in past tense, specific tense changes apply (see table below).
  • vFor universal truths, the Present Indefinite is always used.
  • viAdjectives/adverbs of nearness change to those of distance.
  • viiFor interrogative sentences, reporting verb changes to ask/inquire.
  • viiiFor imperative sentences, reporting verb changes to a word signifying command, precept, or entreaty.

Solved Examples

  • The policeman said to Atul, "This road leads to the station."
    The policeman told Atul that road led to the station.
  • Mother said to Anju, "I am going to the market."
    Mother told Anju that she was going to the market.
  • Ajit said, "Mohit plays cricket."
    Ajit said that Mohit played cricket.
  • Rama said, "Father may reach in the evening."
    Rama said that father might reach in the evening.
  • He said to me, "Have you heard this news?"
    He asked me whether I had heard that news. Interrogative
  • She said to her brother, "Work hard."
    She advised her brother to work hard. Imperative
  • The General said, "March forward, my soldiers."
    The General commanded his soldiers to march forward. Command
  • "Oh! What a fool I have been", he said.
    He exclaimed with regret that he had been a fool. Exclamatory

Transformation: Adverb too
Converting "too…to" into "so…that"
Pattern 1 — too…to → so…that…couldn't
He reached the Bus station too late to catch the bus.
He reached the Bus station so late that he couldn't catch the bus.
Pattern 2 — too good to be true → so good that it cannot be true
This news is too good to be true.
This news is so good that it cannot be true.

Modes of Expressing Contrasting Sentences
Concession and contrast words and phrases
i. though / although / as
He is straightforward, though/although he is rich.
Rich as he is, he is straightforward.
ii. however
However rich he may be, he is never contented.
However often he may try, he will never succeed.
iii. at the same time / all the same
There is some force in what you say; at the same time, we are not in position to take risk.
iv. Participle + noun-clause
Admitting that India may not achieve hundred per cent literacy by 2004, the country may yet come close to the goal.
v. for all that / in spite of
He will not trust you for all that / in spite of all you say in your defence.
vi. although…yet & vii. nevertheless / nonetheless
Although he is very careless in his studies, yet he has got compartment twice.
Nobody blames our cricket team… nevertheless we are all disappointed.

F
Voice: Active ↔ Passive
10 Conditions for Passive Voice Construction
Active-Passive voice transformation pattern
📋 10 Conditions Regarding Passive Voice
  1. 1Only transitive verbs can be changed to passive form.
  2. 2The third form (past participle) of the verb is always used.
  3. 3There must be a helping verb 'be'.
  4. 4Existing helping verbs remain along with 'be' as needed.
  5. 5Tense does not change.
  6. 6Preposition 'by' is generally used when the doer is active.
  7. 7If a preposition already exists, retain it and add our own.
  8. 8The doer is sometimes not written but always implied.
  9. 9Subject takes the place of object and vice versa.
  10. 10The verb is given according to the need of the subject.

Solved Examples

G. Exclamatory ↔ Assertive
Exclamatory → Assertive
If I could only get the Nobel prize!
I earnestly desire to get the Nobel prize.
Exclamatory → Assertive

Negative → Affirmative

Negative → Affirmative

I. Substitution of Parts of Speech
The same idea can be expressed using different parts of speech — noun, verb, adjective, or adverb — without changing the meaning.
FormExample
NounBetween soft skills and hard skills, there is a huge difference of salary.
VerbThe soft skills and hard skills differ greatly in salary.
AdjectiveThe salary offered for soft skills is very different from that for hard skills.
AdverbSoft skills and hard skills very differently command salary.
J. Simple ↔ Compound ↔ Complex
Simple
Notwithstanding his sorrow, he is hopeful.
Compound
He is sorrowful, but yet hopeful.
Simple
He is certain of giving you profit.
Complex
He is certain that he will give you profit.

17.2 Words as Different Parts of Speech
The same word can function as different parts of speech
all

Indef. Num. Adj.

Adj. as Noun
I lost my all on that day.
but
Adverb

Preposition

Conj. Co-ord.
He is man of common sense, but not a good speaker.
near
Adverb
Come near, while I ask you.
Preposition
There is a fine garden near our house.
Adjective
He is a near relative of mine.
since
Preposition
I have not seen him since Sunday.
Adverb
I purchased the house four weeks since.
Conj. Subord.
They must trust you, since you are speaking in earnest.
only
Adjective
The only toy I had was stolen.
Adverb
I heard of this only from you.
Conj. Co-ord.
Do what you like; only.
much
Adv. of Qty.
He has wasted much time.
Adj. of Qty.
I am much pleased with your performance.
Adj. as Noun
He will not get much from you.

Foreign Expressions
Latin, French, Italian, Greek & other borrowed phrases
a la carte
French
According to the bill of fare as given on the menu. We decided to go a la carte.
a posteriori
Latin
What comes after. Inductive reasoning — draws conclusions about causes from their supposed effects. "From what comes after."
a priori
Latin
From the former, already known. Deductive reasoning — works from the general to the particular. "From the former."
ab initio
Latin
From the beginning; especially of contracts which are null and void from the beginning.
ad hoc
Latin
For a particular purpose, without deep thought; temporary. We have made only ad hoc arrangements.
ad interim
Latin
For the meantime, temporary, provisional. Gulzarilal Nanda was appointed ad interim Prime Minister.
alma mater
Latin
One's university or college. The literal meaning is "nourishing mother."
alter ego
Latin
"Other self" — an intimate and trusted friend.
amour vincit omnia
Latin
Love conquers all.
anno domini (A.D.)
Latin
In the year of the Lord. The calendar years before the birth of Jesus are referred to as B.C. (Before Christ).
apartheid
African
"Separation" — the system of rigorous segregation between Europeans and natives practised in South Africa.
cul-de-sac
French
A blind alley. A street or passage that has an entry but not an exit.
de facto
Latin
In actual practice. The things which exist in law do not always exist in fact and vice versa.
ex officio
Latin
In virtue of (his) office. The President of India is ex officio the supreme commander of the armed forces.
fait accompli
French
An accomplished fact. A thing already done. There's no use arguing about it.
paparazzi
Italian
Photographers of celebrities — freelance commercial photographers who stalk celebrities for their pictures.
per diem
Latin
By the day. Paid a daily retainer rather than a regular salary. Lecturers in some coaching classes are paid per diem.
persona non grata
Latin
An unacceptable person; an unwelcome person.
post mortem
Latin
Re-examination of the body to determine the cause of death.
perestroika
Russian
Restructuring — the reform ideas Mikhail Gorbachev brought to the Soviet State in the 1980s.

Modals and Their Modalities
Modal verbs express the meanings/moods of sentences
Modals express the meanings of the sentence — the modality or mood. The various forms or moods of the sentence are: declarative, interrogative, imperative, exclamatory, etc. Modalities include: statement, question, request, ability, wish, permission, possibility, insistence, characteristic, volition.
Overview of 10 modal verbs and their key semantic categories
WILL — 7 modalities
Prediction
I will get a first class this year.
Willingness
I will help you.
Request
Will you get me a pen?
General Facts
Oil will float on water.
Characteristic
He will do anything for money.
Probability
Veni will be in her office now.
Insistence
He will buy this book.
SHALL — 5 modalities
Prediction
I shall see that movie today.
Promise
I shall return this book to you.
Suggestion
Shall we make a move?
Volition
Shall I make some snacks?
Insistence
He shall be taught a lesson.
CAN — 7 modalities
Capability
I can speak Russian.
Permission
You can leave now.
Request
Can you pass the Bill?
Req. for Permission
Can I eat some more ice cream?
Characteristics
Rukmani can talk anything.
Possibility
He can be in the faculty club.
MAY / COULD / MIGHT / WOULD / SHOULD / OUGHT TO
MAY — Permission
You may proceed now.
MAY — Benediction
May you touch greater heights!
MAY — Malediction
May that plunderer be punished!
COULD — Past Capacity
He could play tennis for hours when young.
COULD — Request
Could you carry these few books for me?
MIGHT — Possibility
She might be working still.
WOULD — Future in Past
He said he would do this work for me.
SHOULD — Duty
You should finish their task by Monday.
OUGHT TO — Duty
I ought to meet the principal.
MUST — Obligation
He must listen to what I say.

Phonemic & Phonetic Transcription
Representing pronunciation with symbols
📖 Phonemic Transcription
Also called broad phonetic transcription. Represents pronunciation using sound units (phonemes) without showing distinct variations. Symbols are enclosed in slant lines ( // ).

RP (Received Pronunciation) contains 44 phonemes.
/bɪkʌm/ — 'Become' (phonemic)
📖 Phonetic Transcription
Also called narrow phonetic transcription. Indicates precise phonetic quality using diacritics and special marks. Shows both quality and quantity. Symbols (allophones) are enclosed in square brackets [ ].
[bikɔ̂m] — 'Become' (phonetic/narrow)

17.5 Technical Terms & Their Meanings
Selected A–Z glossary of scientific and engineering vocabulary
Aberration
A disturbance of the rays of a pencil of light such that they can no longer be brought to a sharp focus or form a clear image.
AccelerationMech.
The rate at which the velocity of a moving body increases.
AcousticsPhys.
The science of sound; the study of the effects of sound upon the ear.
AerodynamicsAero.
The branch of science which treats of the motion of air and other gaseous fluids, and of the forces on solids moving in such fluids.
AmpereElec.
The unit of measurement of electric current strength. A compressor of one volt will force one ampere through a resistance of one ohm.
AntibioticChem.
A mold or fungus which kills, retards, or completely represses the growth of other organisms, usually without harming higher orders of life.
Atom
The smallest particle of an element that takes part in a chemical change. It has a nucleus of positive charges called "protons" and revolving negative charges called "electrons."
BatteryElec.
A number of primary or storage cells, condensers, or dynamos grouped together as a single source of electricity.
Chain reaction
A self-sustaining reaction in which the fission of nuclei produces particles that cause the fission of other nuclei.
Compact Disc (CD)
A relatively new type of disc record which needs a special player. The disc is reflective and is read by a laser. The sound is recorded in digital pulses.
Diesel engine
An internal-combustion engine of high efficiency, in which high temperature is obtained through compression; the oil is ignited by the heat developed.
ElectronicsElec.
The science which deals with free electrons, the means of setting them free, their motions when free, and the effects they can produce.
Horsepower
In mechanics, the standard unit by which power is measured. One horsepower is equivalent to 33,000 pounds lifted one foot in one minute.
LASER
Light Amplification by Stimulated Emission of Radiation.
Newton's Laws of MotionPhys.
1st: Every body continues in a state of rest or uniform motion unless compelled. 2nd: Force equals mass × acceleration. 3rd: To every action there is an equal and opposite reaction.
Ohm's LawElec.
The current flowing in a circuit is proportional to the e.m.f. and inversely proportional to the resistance. I = E/R; R = E/I; E = IR
RAM
Random Access Memory — a microchip which can temporarily store digital signals fed in by the user.
ROM
Read Only Memory — a microchip circuit which permanently stores digital signals loaded in by the manufacturer.
WavelengthElec.
The length in meters of one complete sine wave of an alternating current. In radio, the distance in meters between the maximum points of two successive magnetic waves.
X-Ray
Similar to gamma rays, highly penetrating radiation. Not from the nucleus but from surrounding electrons. These rays pass through most objects. Used to photograph shadows of bones and interior organs.
